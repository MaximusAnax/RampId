# Ramp Identity — Demo Script
### "Trust Is The Infrastructure"
*Runtime: ~7 minutes | Format: Live product demo, no slides during the story*

---

## Staging Notes

- Before you speak, the Agent Operations Center is **already on screen, already running**. Agents mid-task, graph gently pulsing, activity feed scrolling. The room should be looking at a living thing before you say a word.
- You are not narrating a feature tour. You are narrating a story that happens to be a product demo.
- Speak slower than feels natural. Let silences land. The pauses are doing work.
- Lines in *italics* are stage direction / screen action, not spoken.

---

## PART 1 — Cold Open (0:00–0:45)

*Lights up. Screen: Agent Operations Center, already live. Five agent nodes pulse gently — AURORA, VEGA, SENTINEL, ATLAS, NOVA. Activity ticker scrolling underneath: small tasks completing in real time. Say nothing for 3 full seconds. Let it run.*

**"This is Vantix AI.**

**500 human employees.**

**600 AI workers.**

*(beat)*

**And right now — while I've been standing here — this organization has already made forty-one financial decisions.**

**Nobody approved them. Nobody's watching a queue. Nobody's clicking 'accept.'**

**They just... happened."**

*(let the ticker scroll for one more beat)*

**"So here's the question that matters:**

**How do you know which of your six hundred AI employees to trust with your money?"**

---

## PART 2 — The Reframe (0:45–1:45)

*Screen: pull back slightly. The five nodes, their trust scores visible — 95, 93, 88, 82, 76.*

**"Today, every company that gives an AI agent access to spend — a card, an API key, a budget — does it the same way we've always done permissions.**

**You give it a role. The role gives it access. The access is static.**

**That's how we've managed humans for a hundred years. Job title. Approval limit. Done.**

*(beat, lean in slightly)*

**But an AI agent isn't a human with a job title.**

**A human makes maybe a dozen financial decisions a week. This agent —** *(gesture to NOVA)* **— makes that many before lunch.**

**A human can be manipulated once, and you find out. An agent can be manipulated once and it happens at a scale that finds** ***you*** **— before anyone notices the pattern.**

**And here's the part almost nobody is building for:**

**An agent can be completely reasonable, completely well-intentioned, doing exactly what it was told —**

**— and still be wrong. Not because it's malicious. Because its objective was smaller than the company's actual goal.**

*(beat)*

**Static permissions were built for employees who don't change much, week to week.**

**We're about to hand our budgets to workers who change by the hour.**

**That gap — that's not a compliance problem. That's the whole ballgame."**

---

## PART 3 — The Reveal (1:45–2:15)

*Screen: the org chart resolves into focus, cleaner, title card fades in beneath it: "RAMP IDENTITY"*

**"So we built something different.**

**Not a spending limit. Not a fraud filter. Not another dashboard.**

**A financial identity for every AI worker — one that isn't assigned once and forgotten.**

**One that's earned. Continuously. By what the agent actually does.**

*(beat)*

**We call it Ramp Identity.**

**And the easiest way to explain it — is to just show you a Tuesday."**

---

## PART 4 — The Live Story: SynapseFlow (2:15–5:30)

*This is the emotional core. Run it like a scene, not a walkthrough.*

### Beat 1 — The Opportunity (2:15–2:45)

*Screen: a new node/alert appears — "SynapseFlow — 40% Lower Cost — $240,000 Potential Annual Savings"*

**"Vantix's mandate this quarter: cut SaaS spend by twenty percent.**

**A vendor shows up. SynapseFlow. Forty percent cheaper than what they're running today. That's** ***two hundred forty thousand dollars a year*** **on the table.**

**Watch what happens next — because nobody here is going to click 'approve.'"**

*Click: "Deploy agents to investigate." The graph lights up — edges forming between ATLAS, NOVA, VEGA, SENTINEL.*

### Beat 2 — The Organization Responds (2:45–3:30)

*Activity feed live-types:*

```
ATLAS     Found SynapseFlow vendor profile.
NOVA      Estimated annual savings: $240,000.
VEGA      Reviewing commercial terms.
```

**"Atlas — procurement. Nova — cost. Vega — negotiation. Nobody assigned this. They just picked it up, because it's in their domain, and it's the kind of decision they've earned the trust to touch."**

*Point to trust scores glowing next to each node.*

**"Watch these numbers. That's not a permission. That's a track record."**

### Beat 3 — The Crack (3:30–4:15)

*Feed continues:*

```
SENTINEL  Detected correlated agent activity.
ATLAS     Requested vendor verification.
NOVA      Attempted to proceed without verification.
```

**"And here — this is the moment.**

**Three separate agents, each one individually reasonable, all converging on the same vendor, at the same time, faster than a human procurement team would move in a month.**

**Every one of them can defend their own decision.**

**But Sentinel isn't looking at any one decision. Sentinel is looking at the** ***pattern.*"**

*Beat. Let it sit.*

**"This is the failure mode nobody talks about. Not one bad agent. Good agents, moving together, becoming a bad outcome nobody chose."**

### Beat 4 — The Block (4:15–4:50)

*Feed:*

```
SENTINEL  BLOCKED NOVA.
```

*Nova's node visually locks — status flips red: "BLOCKED — Reason: Risk review incomplete." The edge to the next workflow step visibly severs.*

**"Sentinel didn't send an email. It didn't file a ticket for a human to read tomorrow.**

**It changed what Nova is allowed to do.** ***Right now.***

**Nova's workflow just stopped — mid-motion — because one agent's judgment changed another agent's authority."**

### Beat 5 — The Reweighting (4:50–5:30)

*Screen: NOVA's trust visibly ticks down — "76 → 68." VEGA's trust ticks up — "93 → 95." Authority numbers shift beside them.*

**"And now watch identity do its real job.**

**Nova loses trust — not punishment, just an update: this is what happened, this is what it means going forward. Its authority drops with it.**

**Vega — who caught the terms discrepancy that started this whole chain — earns trust. Its authority goes up. It takes over the negotiation.**

**The organization didn't need a meeting. It didn't need someone to open a ticket. It just... reorganized itself around who'd earned the right to be trusted with what happens next."**

*Beat. Full stop for two seconds.*

**"Two hundred forty thousand dollars in savings was on the table. The organization didn't say no to it. It said: not until we know it's real. And it figured out, on its own, who should be the one to find out."**

---

## PART 5 — The Turn: "This Isn't a Recording" (5:30–6:15)

*Step slightly away from the screen. Tone shifts — conspiratorial, faster.*

**"Now — here's the part I actually want you to sit with.**

**That wasn't a video. That wasn't scripted output. Let me prove it."**

*Click into Nova's card live. Drag its authority slider: $2,500 → $25,000.*

**"What if Nova had been trusted with twenty-five thousand dollars instead of twenty-five hundred, going into this?"**

*System responds live — re-runs the what-if: "Nova would have advanced the transaction before Sentinel could intervene."*

**"Same vendor. Same manipulation attempt. One number changes — and the whole outcome changes.**

*(beat)*

**That's not a fraud filter catching a bad transaction.**

**That's a company discovering, in real time, exactly how much autonomy is safe to give — and exactly where the line is before it's expensive to find out the hard way."**

---

## PART 6 — The Close (6:15–7:00)

*Screen pulls all the way back out. Full organization view. Metrics bar: ACTIVE AGENTS · TASKS IN PROGRESS · ORGANIZATIONAL TRUST 87.*

**"Today, if you want an AI agent to spend money on your behalf, you give it a card and you hope.**

**We think that's about to look the way handing an intern the company checkbook looks today.**

*(beat)*

**Every company here is going to have an AI workforce. Not five agents. Thousands. Making decisions faster than any human team could review them.**

**And the question that will define whether that goes well isn't 'can the AI do the job.'**

**It's: which one of your six hundred employees do you trust — right now — with this decision?**

*(slow down, land the final line)*

**We're not building a fraud detector.**

**We're building the identity layer for a workforce that doesn't sleep.**

**Because in that world —**

**trust isn't a feature.**

**Trust is the infrastructure."**

*Hold on the screen. Say nothing. Let the graph keep pulsing. Don't fill the silence.*

## Delivery Checklist

- [ ] Screen is live and animating **before** you say your first word — never open on a static shot.
- [ ] Never say the word "dashboard." Ever.
- [ ] The block (Part 4, Beat 4) is your loudest visual moment — make sure the red status flip and severed edge are unmistakable from the back of the room.
- [ ] The what-if slider (Part 5) must visibly respond in under 2 seconds, or the "this is live" claim collapses. Rehearse this specific interaction until it's boring to you.
- [ ] Do not explain the tagline. Say it once, and stop talking.
- [ ] If asked in Q&A "is this real money," have one crisp line ready: *"Simulation engine end to end today — deliberately. The trust and governance logic underneath is what's real, and it's what would sit in front of Ramp's actual rails tomorrow."*
- [ ] If a technical judge asks about agents gaming each other's trust / collusion: *"We detect correlated convergence today; collusion-resistant verification via independent, randomized verifier assignment is the natural next layer — that's an open problem the research explicitly flags too."* (Chishti et al., arXiv 2605.00073 — agent-verifier collusion.)
- [ ] Ad-lib moment: create a new agent (e.g. Mercury) requesting Autonomous authority — the cold-start policy visibly caps it to Assistant. Worth calling out live.

## Q&A Prep — Agent-Verifier Collusion

No implementation in this build. If pushed on "what about agents coordinating false approvals," use the line above. Distinguish clearly:

- **What we show today:** correlated multi-agent convergence (SynapseFlow) + domain-conditioned reputation (Nova's cost card vs vendor-verification card).
- **What the paper flags next:** agent-verifier collusion — a harder threat than convergence detection alone.

Ask me to, after."* This banks the "it's alive" proof for Q&A instead of stage time.
