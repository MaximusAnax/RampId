Compliance evidence business

Decision and build summary · 30 July 2026

The business:

Scan a company's public website three times — untouched, with Global Privacy Control enabled, and after clicking its own reject button — and sell the resulting evidence, then monitor it monthly. Nothing is required from the company, which is what lets it skip the security review that kills unknown vendors.

The metric it was chosen on: profit per hour of your time, not revenue. Two candidates projecting three times the revenue were rejected because delivery hours scale linearly with clients.

What the adversarial validation changed

The plan was attacked by independent researchers and skeptics. These are the corrections that survived verification — the most valuable output of the whole exercise.

The plan saidWhat is actually true
Lead with tracking pixels and lawsuit counts Would have converted at zero. That opening is formally identical to the demand letters plaintiff firms send weekly; counsel train recipients to forward and never reply. Leads now with the § 7025(c)(6) display requirement — same scan, opposite category.
"This urgency cannot be legislated away" Half wrong. SB 690 would retroactively wipe pending § 638.51 claims. But it reaches only §§ 638.50/638.51, § 631 survives, and it is operative January 2027 at the earliest.

Blacklight is the free-tool threat It isn't — source-verified, no GPC or banner interaction at all. I was defending against the wrong tool.

Bugs found, every one of which looked like good news

This is the category that matters, because none of these announce themselves. Each was found by testing against reality rather than against my own fixtures.

  • Chromium silently inherits the system proxy. Behind any corporate proxy, sub-resources fail to load, every tracker looks absent, and every site scans clean.
  • The lead finding accused compliant sites. Consent detection ran at DOMContentLoaded, before any real platform renders its banner. Static fixtures hid it completely; a fixture that renders on a timer now fails without the fix.
  • A bot challenge scored as a perfect clean scan — and in monitoring, a client who started blocking us would have been told every finding was resolved.
  • One failed cycle erased all drift across it, permanently, reported as "unchanged".
  • A new tracker with no new finding ID was filed as "quiet" — the single most common real regression, which would have hollowed out the retainer.
  • Consent verdicts were computed from five retained samples, so a sixth unrestricted request — the only one that mattered — was never examined.

What is built

One command turns a target list into a ranked queue with evidence reports and drafted first messages, scored by how much of your attention each actually deserves:

CommandDoes
campaignTarget list → ranked queue, reports, drafts, sector index
monitorRe-scan, diff, alert only on what moved — the retainer
consentThree-pass evidence for one target
ai50EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure, on the same crawler

Safety is enforced in code, not by discipline: the outreach generator throws on any legal conclusion, ambiguity always resolves to "needs review" rather than an accusation, and a tag that correctly signals consent denied is never reported as a problem.

The one thing not yet known

The engine has never run against a live website. This sandbox blocks arbitrary egress, so detection is validated against fixtures that reproduce real consent-manager markup — but the real-world failure rate is inferred from published surveys, not measured.

Task one, before any outreach: scan 300–500 live domains and measure four numbers. Decision rules are already set — if post-reject failure is under 20% the reject hook is not a business and the GPC pass leads instead; if false positives exceed 2%, send nothing until it is fixed.

Everything else in the plan is downstream of that measurement.
